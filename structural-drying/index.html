Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. After extraction removes the standing water, significant moisture remains trapped in structural materials, including drywall, wood framing, subflooring, concrete, insulation and other building components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Redding Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Leave the drying equipment on even if the noise bothers you, since each hour counts
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
