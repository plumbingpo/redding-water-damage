Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Redding

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Redding finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Stay Clear of ElectricityIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Leave any wet cord or electrical device alone.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. Movement on the dial means water is escaping out of sight, perhaps beneath the slab.
  4. Clear Out and Take PhotosClear out the cabinet or closet near the leak and take photos of stains, warped materials and the pipe itself before anything is repaired or thrown away.
  5. Have the Area MeteredBefore the opening is sealed, get the surrounding materials checked with a moisture meter. A wall that feels dry on the surface can still be wet inside the cavity.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A slow leak and a sudden one are not handled the same way by insurance. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Check your policy and ask your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Leaks at a sink drain joint or trap, which release a little water with every use.
  • Water lines under a concrete slab that get rubbed through or stressed as the soil moves.
  • High water pressure that stresses joints and valves year after year until one weeps.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Pipes punctured by a screw or nail, often during a renovation or when mounting shelves.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Rotted Framing

Studs and the plate beneath them soften and decay when they sit wet for weeks. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Stained Drywall and Ceilings

Leaks over a ceiling or behind a wall surface show as bubbling paint and brown stains. Wet drywall softens and sags over time, and those sections often have to come out.

Mold Inside the Wall

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Batt insulation absorbs a leak and keeps that moisture pressed to the studs. Left in a sealed cavity it can stay wet for a long time, which is why we usually take it out.
